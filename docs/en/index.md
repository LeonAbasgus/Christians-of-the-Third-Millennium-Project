**Leon Adzhindzhal / Hierodeacon Panteleimon**

**Project:** *To Christians of the Third Millennium (CTM)*  
**Document status:** **CANONICAL META-DOCUMENT v1.0**  
**Edition:** Final English Edition  
**Function:** machine-readable conceptual map and authorial normalization of CTM I–III  
**Language:** English

**Principle of precedence:** later explicit methodological formulations in CTM III govern current methodological usage where they refine earlier formulations, without retroactively rewriting the conceptual genealogy of CTM I–II.

## Editorial Protocol

### Statement Provenance

Every substantive statement in this meta-document is governed by one of three provenance statuses.

**CORPUS / CANONICAL** — explicitly present in, or directly established by, CTM I–III.

**AUTHORIAL NORMALIZATION** — a later clarification that resolves terminology, conceptual identity, precedence, or current technical usage without introducing a new research program.

**PROGRAMMATIC EXPLICATION** — an explicit name or synthesis introduced by the author at the meta-level for a research direction materially developed in CTM I–III but not named there in this form.

If no provenance status can be assigned with sufficient confidence, the statement does not belong in this meta-document.

### Non-Expansion Rule

> **If a statement expands CTM rather than identifying, normalizing, or explicitly explicating an already developed structure, it does not belong in this meta-document.**

### Historical Non-Retroactivity

Later normalization establishes current usage but does not retroactively rewrite the historical corpus.

Earlier formulations remain part of the genealogy of the concepts they helped generate. A later methodological formulation may therefore supersede an earlier formulation for current technical use without rendering the earlier formulation erroneous in its historical context.

### Graph Subordination

The Concept Register is derived from this meta-document and from CTM I–III.

No machine-readable relation — including `depends_on`, `part_of`, `supersedes`, `derived_from`, `enables`, or `related_to` — may create a conceptual relation that has not already been established by the corpus or explicitly authorized by this meta-document.

> **The graph records established architecture; it does not generate architecture.**

# 1. What CTM Is

**Provenance: AUTHORIAL NORMALIZATION + CORPUS / CANONICAL**

**To Christians of the Third Millennium (CTM)** is a philosophical-theological project that developed into a **generative research platform** centered on the problem of human subjecthood: its emergence, architecture, realization, distribution, transformation, historical productivity, and eschatological orientation.

CTM did not begin as an attempt to construct a completed general philosophical system. Its conceptual architecture emerged progressively from theological, ontological, anthropological, historical, and methodological inquiry.

**Provenance: AUTHORIAL NORMALIZATION**

A fundamental question running through this development may be formulated as follows:

> **How can a human being who is derivative in relation to a preceding Reality become an actual source of a response of his own?**

**Provenance: CORPUS / CANONICAL**

The human being does not begin himself. His embodiment, origin, language, culture, historical situation, inherited meanings, and many conditions of action precede his freedom.

Derivativeness, however, does not mean that every subsequent human act is exhaustively determined by the causal chain that precedes it.

Subjecthood becomes possible where the human being becomes an actual bearer of a relation of his own to preceding Reality.

**Provenance: AUTHORIAL NORMALIZATION**

Accordingly:

> **Subjecthood is the capacity of a derivative being to become an actual source of a response of his own without claiming sovereignty over the Reality that precedes him.**

Within the present architecture, the following levels must be distinguished:

**CTM** — the project as a whole and the generative research platform that emerged from it.

**Applied Metaphysics of the Subject** — the explicit designation of the central inquiry developed in CTM I.

**The Genesis and Architecture of Subjecthood** — a retrospective meta-description of the central field developed in CTM II; it is not a historical title of the volume.

**CTM Analysis** — the methodology developed in CTM III for reconstructing concrete ontological and subject architectures.

**Vector Analysis of Subjecthood** — the defining analytical core of CTM Analysis.

**Applied Metaphysics of Subjecthood** — a new programmatic designation introduced at the meta-level for the applied field made visible through the conceptual development of CTM I–III.

These designations are related but are not interchangeable.

# 2. The Development of CTM I → II → III

**Provenance: AUTHORIAL NORMALIZATION based on CORPUS / CANONICAL development**

The architecture of CTM was not given in advance. It emerged through successive conceptual expansions and methodological reformulations of the initial problem.

## CTM I — Applied Metaphysics of the Subject

**Provenance: CORPUS / CANONICAL**

CTM I explicitly presents its central inquiry as:

> **An Essay in Applied Metaphysics of the Subject**

Applied metaphysics is not presented there as a speculative construction detached from human existence. It concerns the metaphysical presuppositions from which a human being actually perceives Reality and constructs the trajectory of life, together with the possibility of bringing those presuppositions to examination and verification.

The historical designation **Applied Metaphysics of the Subject** must therefore be preserved.

It must not be retrospectively replaced by *Applied Metaphysics of Subjecthood*.

## CTM II — Genesis and Architecture of Subjecthood

**Provenance: AUTHORIAL NORMALIZATION for the heading; CORPUS / CANONICAL for the described development**

CTM II systematically develops a problem already present in CTM I:

> **subjecthood is not automatically given.**

It develops an account of the emergence, ontogenesis, architecture, historical realization, projectuality, preservation, loss, and eschatological orientation of subjecthood.

A major sequence of inquiry may be represented as:

> **Genesis → Quality of Ontology → Ontogenesis of Subjecthood → Eschatology**

CTM II also extends the conceptual apparatus into historical and social inquiry.

Modernization is explicitly identified as the first practical application of CTM.

The genealogy of ontologies opens a further research horizon without yet constituting a completed independent theory.

## CTM III — Methodological Normalization

**Provenance: CORPUS / CANONICAL**

CTM III transforms distinctions generated through the preceding development into a reproducible methodology for reconstructing ontological and subject architectures.

Its defining formula is:

> **CTM analysis is the vector analysis of the level, direction, bearer, and distribution of subjecthood.**

Its primary object is not the declared identity of a phenomenon but the architecture of response and subjecthood actually produced within it.

**Provenance: AUTHORIAL NORMALIZATION**

CTM III therefore represents not a replacement of CTM I–II but their methodological normalization and operational development.

Where CTM III explicitly refines an earlier technical formulation, the later formulation governs current methodological usage while the earlier formulation remains part of the genealogy of the concept.

# 3. Applied Metaphysics of Subjecthood

## Status

**Provenance: PROGRAMMATIC EXPLICATION**

**Applied Metaphysics of Subjecthood** is introduced in this meta-document as a new programmatic designation.

It is materially derived from the conceptual development of CTM I–III but is not the historical title of CTM I and is not presented in the existing corpus as the established name of an autonomous discipline.

## Genealogy

**Provenance: CORPUS / CANONICAL + AUTHORIAL NORMALIZATION**

CTM I explicitly develops an **Applied Metaphysics of the Subject**.

CTM II shifts the analytical center toward the genesis, ontogenesis, architecture, projectuality, historical realization, and eschatological orientation of **subjecthood**.

CTM III develops a methodology for reconstructing **operative ontologies and subject architectures** and for analyzing the level, direction, bearer, and distribution of subjecthood.

The designation **Applied Metaphysics of Subjecthood** names the research field that becomes visible through this development.

## Object

**Provenance: PROGRAMMATIC EXPLICATION**

Its central object is the relation:

> **ontological architecture ↔ subjecthood**

## Central Question

**Provenance: PROGRAMMATIC EXPLICATION constrained by CORPUS terminology**

> **How does an ontological architecture configure the possibilities of human subjecthood, and how does a subject appropriate, reproduce, correct, transform, or generate that architecture through a response of his own?**

Applied Metaphysics of Subjecthood is therefore not merely the application of a ready-made metaphysical doctrine to empirical material.

It reconstructs the **operative ontological architecture** functioning in concrete human action and investigates its relation to the realization, distribution, reproduction, correction, and transformation of subjecthood.

## Methodological Relation

**Provenance: AUTHORIAL NORMALIZATION**

Applied Metaphysics of Subjecthood is not identical with CTM Analysis.

CTM Analysis provides the developed methodology through which many questions belonging to this field can be investigated.

Vector Analysis of Subjecthood is the defining analytical core of CTM Analysis.

Thus:

> **Applied Metaphysics of Subjecthood ≠ CTM Analysis ≠ Vector Analysis of Subjecthood**

The first designates a programmatically explicated research field.

The second designates a developed methodology.

The third designates the defining analytical core of that methodology.

# 4. Reality and Map

**Provenance: CORPUS / CANONICAL**

CTM begins from the distinction:

> **Reality ≠ map**

Human action does not proceed from Reality without mediation.

Reality becomes available to a subject through perception, memory, language, images, concepts, distinctions, causal models, narratives, expectations, and evaluations.

These may form a map of Reality, but the map does not exhaust Reality.

The human map is finite.

Its finitude does not imply the finitude of Reality.

A map may correspond to Reality more or less adequately and may be corrected through encounter with what exceeds it.

**Provenance: AUTHORIAL NORMALIZATION**

The distinction between Reality and map makes verification, correction, learning, and responsible response intelligible within the analytical architecture of CTM.

# 5. Operative Ontology

## Working Concept

**Provenance: CORPUS / CANONICAL**

In the methodological context of CTM III, ontology means neither the academic discipline concerned with being nor an exhaustive metaphysical picture of the world.

**Operative ontology** is the operative architecture of Reality accessible to a subject, within which something can be recognized as:

- existing;
- significant;
- causally connected;
- possible;
- requiring a response.

The object of analysis is therefore not merely a set of beliefs but the architecture of the world within which beliefs acquire meaning and operational force.

**Provenance: AUTHORIAL NORMALIZATION**

In this formulation, **“Reality accessible to a subject” does not mean a directly given portion of Reality itself**. It designates Reality insofar as it becomes present within the subject’s operative architecture. The distinction **Reality ≠ map** therefore remains fully in force.

DIS is accordingly not a portion of Reality itself. It is the space within which Reality becomes present to the subject.

## DIS × OS × Subjecthood

**Provenance: CORPUS / CANONICAL**

CTM III analyzes operative ontology through three interdependent dimensions:

> **DIS × OS × subjecthood**

The multiplication sign does not represent arithmetic multiplication.

It indicates **non-additive interdependence**: the functioning of the whole configuration cannot be reconstructed adequately by treating the three dimensions as independent additive components.

## Dynamic Iconic Space — DIS

**Provenance: CORPUS / CANONICAL**

**Dynamic Iconic Space (DIS)** is the configuration of Reality held by a subject: what is present as actual, significant, causally connected, and capable of requiring a response.

Its primary analytical question is:

> **What Reality is available to the subject?**

## Operating System — OS

**Provenance: CORPUS / CANONICAL**

**Operating System (OS)** is the repertoire and organization of ways in which the subject can interact with the Reality made available within DIS.

Its primary question is:

> **What can the subject do with what has become available?**

## Subjecthood

**Provenance: CORPUS / CANONICAL**

The subjecthood dimension concerns the actual bearer and organization of response.

Its primary question is:

> **Who can actually respond?**

It concerns who may recognize, interpret, begin, participate, object, correct, stop, and assume responsibility.

**Provenance: AUTHORIAL NORMALIZATION**

Thus:

> **DIS ≠ OS ≠ subjecthood**

None of the three independently exhausts operative ontology.

CTM III therefore provides a methodological refinement of earlier CTM II formulations such as *ontology as DIS*, *ontology as an Operating System*, and *ontology as subjecthood*.

The earlier formulations remain part of the genealogy of the model.

# 6. Scenario Book

**Provenance: CORPUS / CANONICAL**

The **Scenario Book** is a relatively stable system of rules for the situational linkage of DIS, OS, and subjecthood into a unified configuration of an **available response**.

It is not postulated as an independent psychic entity or an additional ontological substance.

Its existence must be reconstructible through stable features of the transition from situation to available response.

**Provenance: AUTHORIAL NORMALIZATION**

Three analytical levels must be distinguished:

> **Scenario Book ≠ scenario ≠ response**

The **Scenario Book** contains relatively stable rules of situational assembly.

A **scenario** is a concrete situational configuration generated through those rules.

A **response** is the subject’s own act that may realize, modify, stop, or transform the available configuration.

Therefore:

> **available response ≠ realized response**

The Scenario Book does not respond instead of the subject.

**Provenance: AUTHORIAL NORMALIZATION of conceptual genealogy**

The CTM III formulation governs current methodological usage.

The narrower formulations developed in CTM II remain part of the historical genealogy of the concept.

# 7. Subject Architecture

**Provenance: CORPUS / CANONICAL**

**Subject architecture** is the configuration of bearers, powers, and relations that determines who is capable of:

- recognizing;
- interpreting;
- beginning;
- participating;
- objecting;
- correcting;
- stopping;
- assuming responsibility.

Subject architecture allows CTM to move from declarations about freedom, participation, authority, and responsibility to their operational distribution.

A system may distribute recognition, decision, execution, correction, stopping, responsibility, and cost among different bearers.

The existence of activity does not by itself demonstrate the location of authorship.

A formal attribution of authorship does not by itself establish the actual bearer of response.

A central analytical distinction is therefore:

> **formal author ≠ actual bearer**

The analysis reconstructs the **actual distribution of subjecthood**.

# 8. CTM Analysis

**Provenance: CORPUS / CANONICAL**

**CTM Analysis** is the methodology for reconstructing concrete ontological and subject architectures.

Its defining formula is:

> **CTM analysis is the vector analysis of the level, direction, bearer, and distribution of subjecthood.**

CTM Analysis investigates not merely what a phenomenon declares itself to be, but what architecture of subjecthood it actually produces and where that architecture leads.

Its questions include:

- What Reality becomes available?
- What map mediates that Reality?
- What operations become possible?
- Who recognizes?
- Who interprets?
- Who begins?
- Who participates?
- Who may object?
- Who may correct?
- Who may stop?
- Who bears responsibility?
- Who bears the cost?
- Where is actual authorship located?
- What kind of subjecthood is produced?
- How does that configuration change over time?

A developed analysis may include:

> reconstruction of preceding Reality → reconstruction of the map → reconstruction of the Operating System → establishment of the actual distribution of subjecthood → subject topography → subject topology and connectedness → reconstruction of the Scenario Book → compatibility checks → vectorographic analysis → mode of correspondence → cost analysis → alternative reconstruction → negative verification → final epistemic result.

This sequence is not an irreversible algorithm.

Later evidence may require returning to an earlier analytical stage.

# 10. Projectuality and Generativity

**Provenance: CORPUS / CANONICAL**

**Projectuality** is the capacity to organize a response of one’s own in relation to the future.

It is not reducible to the existence of plans or declared goals.

**Generativity** concerns a stronger transformation.

It becomes analytically relevant where the subject’s own beginning changes the architecture of what can be begun next.

CTM III gives the distinction in compressed form:

> **subjecthood — the capacity to begin while remaining one who has been begun;**

> **generativity — the capacity, through one’s own beginning, to change the architecture of what can be begun next.**

Accordingly:

> **subjecthood ≠ projectuality ≠ generativity**

A subject may give a response of his own within an already existing architecture without generating a new architecture of possible responses.

---

# 11. Ontological Solvency and Ontological Creditworthiness

**Provenance: CORPUS / CANONICAL**

**Ontological solvency** is the actual capacity to accept the cost and consequences of one’s own authorship.

It concerns the capacity actually to bear the price of response.

**Ontological creditworthiness** concerns the justified expectation of connectedness among promise, action, outcome, verification, and responsibility.

The two concepts are therefore distinct:

> **ontological solvency ≠ ontological creditworthiness**

Solvency concerns actual capacity to bear cost.

Creditworthiness concerns the justified expectation that authorship and responsibility will remain connected across action and consequence.

# 12. Operative Ontology, Operational Ontology, and Operating System

**Provenance: AUTHORIAL NORMALIZATION based on distinct corpus usages**

These terms must not be merged.

## Operative Ontology

**Operative ontology** is the methodological concept developed in CTM III for the actually functioning architecture of Reality accessible to a subject.

## Operational Ontology

**Operational ontology** is the concept developed in CTM II for the local ontology associated with a differentiated social or professional function.

Operational ontologies may arise in connection with specialized roles, practices, institutions, and forms of activity.

## Operating System

**Operating System** is one of the three interdependent dimensions through which CTM III analyzes operative ontology.

It concerns the organization of available operations in relation to the Reality represented within DIS.

Therefore:

> **operative ontology ≠ operational ontology ≠ Operating System**

This distinction is mandatory for current English technical usage.

# 13. Domain-Specific Research and Applications

**Provenance: CORPUS / CANONICAL**

CTM II explicitly identifies **modernization theory** as the first practical application of CTM.

It also states that the conceptual apparatus developed by CTM possesses a generative capacity extending beyond the original object of inquiry.

Application to new fields may generate new objects, new research questions, and new specialized investigations.

**Provenance: CORPUS / CANONICAL**

CTM III explicitly states that CTM does not replace specialized humanistic disciplines.

The historian, jurist, sociologist, psychologist, economist, theologian, and other specialists retain the specificity of their own objects and methods.

CTM begins its work where the results of specialized disciplines must be assembled into a common reconstruction of relations among:

> **Reality — map — architecture of the possible — response — responsibility**

Its metalinguistic function is therefore non-reductionist.

CTM does not claim that all human phenomena are “really” one substance such as subjecthood.

It asks, at another analytical level, what architecture of Reality, response, authorship, responsibility, and subjecthood is present in a given object.

**Provenance: AUTHORIAL NORMALIZATION**

Further domain-specific applications remain open.

They are **not defined in this meta-document**.

# 15. Negative Verification and Fail-Closed

## Negative Verification

**Provenance: CORPUS / CANONICAL**

CTM permits a negative analytical result.

The absence of evidence required for a necessary transition does not authorize the analyst to treat that transition as established.

The methodological rule is:

> **no transition without a demonstrated transition**

This principle applies wherever an analytical conclusion requires proof of continuity, compatibility, preservation, causality, scale transfer, temporal extension, subject identity, or another necessary relation.

If the required transition has not been demonstrated, the connection remains a hypothesis.

## Fail-Closed

**Provenance: CORPUS / CANONICAL for specific use; AUTHORIAL NORMALIZATION for general formulation**

In CTM, **fail-closed** designates the blocking of an inferential or operative continuation when a required condition of applicability or transition has not been demonstrated.

It does not determine the alternative outcome.

As an epistemic rule:

> **an undemonstrated necessary transition may not be treated as established.**

In scenario analysis, when relevant discrepancy information appears, the absence of demonstrated preservation of applicability conditions blocks automatic continuation of the previous scenario assembly.

The blocking of the previous assembly opens space for **renewed recognition, redistribution of powers, and formation of the next scenario**.

Thus fail-closed does not prove what happens instead.

It prevents unauthorized continuation.
